How it works

Your employer must pay 12% of your salary into super (the super guarantee), up to the maximum contribution base of $270,830 for 2026-27. With Payday Super this is now a yearly limit, so the most an employer must pay is $32,499.60. Salary you sacrifice still counts, so sacrificing doesn't reduce your employer's 12%.

Salary sacrifice means asking your employer to pay part of your pre-tax salary into super. It isn't taxed at your marginal rate. Instead your fund takes 15% contributions tax. If your marginal rate plus the 2% Medicare levy is more than 15%, you save the difference. On $100,000, sacrificing $10,000 saves $3,200 in income tax and Medicare, costs $1,500 in contributions tax, and cuts take-home pay by $6,800. That puts $8,500 more in super.

Employer, salary sacrifice and deductible personal contributions all count towards the concessional contributions cap of $32,500 from 1 July 2026. Amounts over the cap are added to your taxable income and taxed at your marginal rate, less a 15% offset, so they save little. If your total super balance was under $500,000 on 30 June last year, you can use unused cap amounts from the past 5 years. myGov shows how much you have.

If your income plus concessional contributions is over $250,000, Division 293 adds another 15% on the smaller of your contributions or the amount over $250,000.

Frequently asked questions

Is salary sacrifice worth it?

Usually, if your taxable income is over $45,000. There your tax rate is 30% or more plus the 2% Medicare levy, against 15% in super. Below about $45,000 the saving is small or nothing, and the money is locked away until you can access your super.

What is the concessional contributions cap for 2026-27?

$32,500, up from $30,000. It covers your employer's super guarantee, salary sacrifice and personal contributions you claim as a tax deduction, across all your funds.

How do I find my unused carry-forward cap?

Log in to myGov, open the ATO, then Super, Manage, and Carry-forward concessional contributions. You can only use it if your total super balance was under $500,000 on 30 June of the previous year. Unused amounts expire after 5 years.

What happens if I go over the cap?

The excess is added to your taxable income and taxed at your marginal rate, with a 15% offset for the tax your fund already paid. The ATO writes to you with options for paying it.

What is Division 293 tax?

An extra 15% on concessional contributions for people whose income plus contributions is over $250,000. It applies to the smaller of your contributions or the amount over $250,000. The ATO bills you separately, and you can pay it from your super.
