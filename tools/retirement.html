How it works

Checked 15 Oct 2026. Defaults follow Moneysmart's superannuation calculator; ASFA standard as published by Moneysmart.

The calculator projects your super year by year to your retirement age. Each year your employer pays 12% of your salary (up to the maximum contribution base), plus any salary sacrifice. Your fund takes 15% contributions tax. Salary sacrifice is cut back if your employer super plus sacrifice would go over the $32,500 concessional cap. Earnings are taxed at an assumed effective 7%, then fees come off. Salary grows 3.7% a year by default (Moneysmart's 2.5% inflation plus 1.2% for rising living standards).

Results are in today's dollars: future balances are divided by inflation (2.5% by default) so you can compare them with prices now. With the defaults (age 35, $80,000 in super, $90,000 salary), you'd retire at 67 with about $827,116 in today's dollars, or $1,822,763 in 2058 dollars. Moneysmart also takes off the 1.2% for rising living standards, which would show $569,915.

In retirement we draw your chosen income at the start of each year. The balance keeps earning the same return after fees, with no tax on earnings (an assumption for a retirement-phase pension). The ASFA Retirement Standard (June quarter 2026) puts a comfortable retirement at $56,166 a year for a single homeowner and $78,998 for a couple, and a modest one at $36,548 and $52,690. The Age Pension (from age 67) isn't included, so super alone runs out sooner than your full retirement income would.

Frequently asked questions

How much super do I need to retire comfortably?

ASFA says $630,000 for a single homeowner and $730,000 for a couple at 67, in today's dollars. That assumes you also get a part Age Pension and spend the whole balance. On its own, without the pension, $630,000 drawing $56,166 a year lasts about 14 years here.

How long will my super last?

It depends on the income you draw and your returns. With the default inputs, $827,116 at 67 drawing ASFA's comfortable $56,166 a year lasts about 20 years, to age 87, before any Age Pension.

How much difference does salary sacrifice make?

With the defaults, an extra $5,000 a year (rising with wages) adds about $278,063 in today's dollars by 67, after the 15% contributions tax. Your employer super plus sacrifice must stay within the $32,500 concessional cap.

Do fees really matter?

Yes. With the defaults, an extra 0.5% a year in fees leaves you about $80,047 less at 67 in today's dollars. Compare your fund's fees and costs summary with Moneysmart's defaults of $59 a year plus 0.11%.

When can I get my super and the Age Pension?

You can usually take your super from 60 (your preservation age, if you were born after 30 June 1964) once you retire, or at 65 even if you keep working. The Age Pension starts at 67 and is means-tested. Check your rate with Services Australia.
