How it works

A capital gain is what you sell an asset for, less its cost base: the price plus buying costs, improvements and selling costs. The taxable part is added to your income for the year and taxed at your marginal rate, plus the Medicare levy. Your home is exempt.

Until 30 June 2027, Australian residents who've held an asset for more than 12 months pay tax on only half the gain (the 50% CGT discount).

From 1 July 2027, the Treasury Laws Amendment (Tax Reform No. 1) Act 2026 replaces the discount with two rules:

  • Indexation: the cost base grows with inflation (CPI, quarter by quarter, to 3 decimal places), so only the real gain is taxed. Indexation can reduce a gain to nil but can't create a loss.
  • A 30% minimum tax: if your marginal rate on the gain is below 30%, you pay a top-up so the gain from 1 July 2027 is taxed at 30% at least (Medicare levy not counted). People receiving income support, such as the Age Pension or JobSeeker, are exempt.

Held across 1 July 2027? The asset is treated as sold and bought back at its value on that date. The gain to then keeps the 50% discount; growth after it is indexed and minimum-taxed. If you don't have a valuation, Treasury's draft method estimates one by assuming steady, compounding growth over the whole time you owned it.

Newly built residential property can choose the 50% discount or indexation; this calculator picks whichever costs less. Foreign and temporary residents get neither the discount nor indexation.

Frequently asked questions

Does the new CGT apply to shares I already own?

Only to growth from 1 July 2027. Gains up to 30 June 2027 keep the 50% discount when you eventually sell, if you've held the asset for more than 12 months in total. For listed shares, the value at 30 June 2027 is their market price that day.

Is the new CGT law passed?

Yes. The Treasury Laws Amendment (Tax Reform No. 1) Act 2026 received Royal Assent on 26 June 2026. Some details are still being finalised, including the definition of a new build and the apportioning method, so the calculator says when it relies on them.

Will I pay more tax under the new rules?

It depends on inflation, how long you hold the asset and your income. Growth well above inflation is taxed more than under the 50% discount; slow growth in high inflation can be taxed less. The 30% minimum tax mainly affects people with lower incomes, such as retirees not on the Age Pension. The calculator shows both results side by side.

Why do I need to enter my other income?

Capital gains are added to your taxable income, so the tax depends on your marginal rate. A $50,000 gain costs much less for someone with no other income than for someone earning $200,000.

What isn't covered?

Partial main residence exemptions, small business concessions, assets bought before 21 September 1999 using frozen indexation, trusts and companies, and the Working Australians Tax Offset. Ask a registered tax agent if these apply to you.
