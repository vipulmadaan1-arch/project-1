How it works

Checked 15 Oct 2026. Repayments use the same formula as Moneysmart's mortgage calculator, with interest charged at your repayment frequency.

On a $600,000 loan at 6% over 30 years, keeping $50,000 in offset saves $202,175 in interest and finishes the loan 4 years 8 months sooner. $100,000 in offset saves $338,658 and 7 years 10 months. Both assume the balance stays the same and you keep paying $3,597 a month.

An offset account is a transaction account linked to your home loan. Its balance is subtracted from your loan balance before interest is calculated, so $50,000 in offset on a $600,000 loan means you're charged interest on $550,000.

Because your repayment stays the same, more of each payment goes to principal and the loan finishes early. This calculator compares the loan with and without the offset to show interest and time saved.

Frequently asked questions

Is an offset better than extra repayments?

Financially they save the same interest. An offset keeps your money accessible; extra repayments may be harder to withdraw (redraw rules vary by lender).

Does the calculator assume my offset balance changes?

No, it assumes a constant balance. In practice your balance moves with your pay and spending.

Is offset interest saving taxable?

Interest saved through an offset isn't income, so it isn't taxed, unlike interest earned in a savings account. Check your own situation with a tax professional.
