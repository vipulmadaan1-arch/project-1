How it works

Each state and territory sets its own transfer (stamp) duty on property purchases, charged on the dutiable value: usually the higher of the price and the market value. Rates step up in brackets, and most states charge less if you'll live in the home or are buying your first home.

Foreign buyers pay the surcharge on the full dutiable value, on top of duty and before any concession. First home relief needs at least one buyer who is an Australian citizen or permanent resident in every state except the ACT and NT, and from 1 August 2026 Queensland's home concession does too. With a joint purchase the surcharge applies only to the foreign buyer's share.

Frequently asked questions

Which state has the cheapest stamp duty?

It depends on price and who's buying. On a $750,000 investment property the duty is $22,200 in the ACT, $26,775 in Queensland, $27,937 in NSW, $28,935 in Tasmania, $29,741 in WA, $35,080 in South Australia, $37,125 in the NT and $40,070 in Victoria. First home buyers can pay nothing in several states, depending on price and whether the home is new.

Why does the new home box matter?

In Queensland and South Australia, first home buyers pay no duty on a new home regardless of price, but get less or no relief on an established home. In the NT, anyone buying a new house and land package from a builder to live in pays no duty, for contracts signed to 30 June 2027.

Can foreign buyers get first home buyer relief?

Not on their own, except in the ACT. NSW, Victoria, Queensland, South Australia, WA and Tasmania need at least one buyer to be an Australian citizen or permanent resident (New Zealand citizens on a special category visa usually count too). If you buy with one, the surcharge applies only to the foreign buyer's share, so check the exact figure with your state's revenue office.

Are any figures estimates?

Yes. Victoria's and NSW's first home buyer tapers, and NT rates above $3 million, use formulas published by independent calculators because the revenue offices don't publish them. The page tells you when a result uses one.

Does this cover commercial property or vacant land?

No. It covers residential purchases. Vacant land and commercial property often have different rules; check your state's revenue office.
