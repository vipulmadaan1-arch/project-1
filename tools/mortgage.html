How it works

Checked 15 Oct 2026. Uses the same approach as Moneysmart's mortgage calculator, so you can cross-check a result there.

Repayments on a principal and interest loan are calculated with the standard amortisation formula: each repayment covers that period's interest and pays down some principal. Early on, most of each repayment is interest.

Frequently asked questions

What are the repayments on a $600,000 mortgage?

$3,597 a month at 6% p.a. over 30 years, or $1,659.50 a fortnight or $829.58 a week. At 5.5% it's $3,407 a month and at 6.5% it's $3,792.

How much interest will I pay over 30 years?

$695,029 on a $600,000 loan at 6% p.a. with monthly repayments, so you repay $1,295,029 in total. At 5.5% the interest is $626,424 and at 6.5% it's $765,267. In the first year, $35,800 of the $43,168 you repay is interest.

Does this include fees or LMI?

No. It covers principal and interest only, at a constant rate. Lender fees, lenders mortgage insurance and rate changes aren't included.

What about interest-only loans?

Interest-only repayments are simply loan amount × rate ÷ repayments per year. This calculator covers principal and interest loans.

How much will I save with an offset account?

About $202,175 in interest and 4 years 8 months off the loan, with $50,000 in offset against a $600,000 loan at 6% over 30 years and the same $3,597 monthly repayment. Use the offset account calculator for your own balance.
