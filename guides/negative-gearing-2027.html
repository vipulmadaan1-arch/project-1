Negative gearing from 1 July 2027: who is caught

18 November 2026

Until now, a rental loss on an investment property has reduced the tax on your wages. From the 2027-28 income year that stops for most residential property bought after Budget night 2026. The rule is section 26-155 of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1997, added by the Treasury Laws Amendment (Tax Reform No. 1) Act 2026 (legislation.gov.au).

Who isn't caught

  • Property you bought before 7.30 pm AEST on 12 May 2026. The time you signed the contract is what counts, even if settlement came later.
  • New builds. These stay fully negatively gearable. The exact definition is in the Treasury Laws Amendment (Tax Reform No. 3) Bill 2026, which hadn't passed the Senate as at 18 November 2026. The Budget papers describe a home built on vacant land, or one that replaces demolished housing with more homes, bought from new.
  • Shares, commercial property and other investments. Only residential property is affected.

Everyone else is caught from 1 July 2027. In 2026-27 a loss on a property bought after Budget night still reduces tax on your salary as before.

A worked example

A property bought for $750,000 with a $600,000 interest-only loan at 7%. Cotality says investor rates are now close to that level. It rents for $650 a week and is empty for two weeks a year. The owner earns $120,000.

  • Bought before Budget night: the loss cuts tax on salary by $5,528 (30% tax plus 2% Medicare levy), so the property costs about $226 a week to hold.
  • Bought after Budget night: from 2027-28 there's no tax saving that year, so it costs about $332 a week. That's about $5,500 a year more.
  • A new build with $8,000 a year of depreciation: a $25,275 loss for tax saves $8,088, so it costs about $177 a week.

The same property bought after Budget night still saves $5,528 in 2026-27. The difference starts from 1 July 2027. Try your own figures in the rental cashflow calculator.

What happens to the loss

It isn't lost. It's carried forward with no time limit, and it can reduce:

  • rental profit from your caught properties in later years, and
  • capital gains when you sell residential property.

It can never reduce tax on wages or business income. In the example, if a later year brings a $3,650 rental profit, the carried loss wipes out the tax on it and $13,625 carries on. When the property is sold, what's left comes off the capital gain.

If you own more than one caught property, the limit applies to them together: a profit on one absorbs a loss on another in the same year.

If you're buying now

  • Work out the weekly cost with no tax saving. If you can't carry that, the purchase depends on the price rising.
  • If you're buying a new build to keep negative gearing, check the developer's claim against the final definition once the bill passes.
  • Remember the capital gains tax changes on the same date: indexation and a 30% minimum tax replace the 50% discount (see four worked examples).
