What the November 2026 rate rise does to your home loan

18 November 2026

On 3 November 2026 the Reserve Bank lifted the cash rate by 0.25 percentage points to 4.85%, its fifth increase this year (RBA media release). The four earlier rises had already added a full percentage point, so the cash rate is now 1.25 points higher than in January.

Most lenders pass a cash rate rise on to variable home loans within a few weeks. Your lender will tell you the new rate and repayment, and that's the number to go by. As a guide, Cotality expects the average owner-occupier variable rate to be about 6.7% once this rise flows through (Cotality Home Value Index, November 2026; an industry estimate, not an official figure).

Your repayments

The table assumes a 30-year principal and interest loan with monthly repayments, and that your lender passed on every rise in full. 6.45% is the rate before this rise, and 5.45% is the same loan in January, before any of this year's five rises.

The November rise on its own adds $66 a month on $400,000, $99 on $600,000 and $132 on $800,000. At these rates each 0.25-point rise costs about $16 a month for every $100,000 you owe. If your loan has less than 30 years left, the rise in dollars is a little smaller, because there's less interest left to pay.

How much you can borrow

Banks must check you could still repay if your rate were 3 percentage points higher (APRA's serviceability buffer). So a 6.7% loan is assessed at 9.7%, and every rise cuts the largest loan a bank will offer.

  • One person on $100,000 with $2,500 a month of living costs and no other debts: about $517,000 in January, about $463,000 now.
  • A couple on $100,000 and $80,000 with $4,000 a month of living costs: about $1,016,000 in January, about $909,000 now.

That's roughly 10.5% less borrowing power in ten months, close to Cotality's estimate of about 11.5% for the whole run of rises. Over the same period Cotality's national median dwelling value fell about $63,400 from its March peak, so for most buyers the smaller loan has cancelled out the lower prices. These are estimates from the borrowing power calculator. Each lender uses its own living expense figures, and most will lend somewhat less.

Things worth checking this month

Your new repayment. If the repayment comes out by direct debit, the lender changes it for you. If you pay by a regular transfer you set up yourself, update the amount, or you'll fall behind.

Your offset balance. Money in an offset account saves interest at your loan rate, tax free. At 6.7%, every $10,000 sitting in offset for a year saves about $670 of interest. The offset calculator shows what a steady balance saves over the whole loan.

Whether your rate is competitive. Lenders often charge existing customers more than new ones. If you're well above the rates advertised for new loans, ask your lender to match them before you think about moving. If you do move, the refinance calculator works out how long the switching costs take to pay back.

A fixed rate ending soon. A loan fixed in 2023 or 2024 rolls onto your lender's variable rate when the fixed term ends. Find out what that rate will be a few months ahead, so you aren't comparing lenders in a hurry.

What happens next

Nobody knows. The Reserve Bank board was split six votes to three on this rise. Its next decision is due in December. If you want to see how a further rise would hit you, put 6.95% or 7.2% into the mortgage calculator now.
