Capital gains tax from 1 July 2027: four worked examples

18 November 2026

From 1 July 2027 the 50% capital gains tax discount ends for most assets. In its place the cost of an asset is indexed to inflation, so only the real gain is taxed, and the gain from 1 July 2027 is taxed at no less than 30%. The law is passed: the Treasury Laws Amendment (Tax Reform No. 1) Act 2026 received Royal Assent on 26 June 2026 (legislation.gov.au).

Your own home stays exempt. The changes hit shares, crypto and investment property.

The rules are easier to follow with numbers, so here are four cases. Each was run through the capital gains tax calculator. Each assumes an Australian resident, uses the legislated 2027-28 tax rates for sales from July 2027, includes the Medicare levy, and projects inflation at 2.5% a year after the latest published CPI. Actual inflation will change the results.

1. Shares bought before July 2027 and sold after

Bought on 1 March 2021 for $50,000, worth $80,000 on 30 June 2027, sold on 1 March 2030 for $100,000. Other income: $90,000.

Anything you own on 30 June 2027 is treated as sold and bought back at its value that day. The $30,000 of growth up to then keeps the 50% discount, so $15,000 of it is taxed. The $20,000 after it is reduced by indexation. The $80,000 cost base is indexed by a factor of 1.064 (CPI growth over the period) to $85,120, which leaves $14,880 to tax.

The taxable gain is $29,880 and the tax $9,562. If the old rules still applied to the whole $50,000 gain, the tax would be $8,000.

For listed shares the 30 June 2027 value is simply the closing price that day, so it's worth saving a copy of your holding statement then.

2. A retiree selling shares

Bought on 1 July 2027 for $60,000, sold on 1 July 2030 for $100,000. Other taxable income: $30,000. Not receiving the Age Pension or other income support.

Indexation (a factor of 1.077) lifts the cost base to $64,620, so the taxable gain is $35,380. Ordinary tax on it, including the Medicare levy, is $10,003.

Then the minimum tax applies. Part of the gain falls in the 14% bracket, so ordinary income tax on it, before offsets and the Medicare levy, is only $8,214. 30% of $35,380 is $10,614, so there's a $2,400 top-up. The total is $12,403.

Under the old rules the same sale would have cost $4,851. The minimum tax is aimed squarely at people on lower incomes with large gains. People receiving income support such as the Age Pension or JobSeeker in the year of the sale don't pay it.

3. An investment property bought in 2015

Bought on 1 July 2015 for $600,000, plus $30,000 of stamp duty and buying costs. Sold on 1 July 2032 for $1,000,000, with $20,000 of selling costs. Other income: $100,000. No valuation at 30 June 2027.

Without a valuation, Treasury's draft method assumes the property grew at one steady rate over the 17 years, which puts its 30 June 2027 value at about $860,412. The $230,412 of growth to then keeps the discount, so $115,206 is taxed. After that the indexed cost base is $973,126 and the net sale price $980,000, leaving a gain of only $6,875.

Total taxable gain: $122,080. Tax: $47,728. Under the old rules it would be $72,600.

The new rules come out cheaper here because most of the growth happened before July 2027, and indexation removes nearly all of the rest. If the property grows much faster than inflation after 2027, the result flips. A valuation at 30 June 2027 replaces the steady-growth assumption with a real figure. Ask a valuer about one closer to the date.

4. A newly built investment property

Bought new on 1 September 2027 for $700,000, sold on 1 September 2032 for $900,000. Other income: $100,000.

New residential builds can choose either the 50% discount or indexation with the minimum tax. Here the discount is cheaper: $100,000 is taxed and the tax is $37,350, the same as under the old rules.

What counts as a new build isn't settled. The definition is in the Treasury Laws Amendment (Tax Reform No. 3) Bill 2026, which was introduced on 14 October 2026. A Senate committee reported on it on 13 November 2026, and as at 18 November it hadn't passed the Senate.

What to do before July 2027

  • Keep records of what you paid, including buying costs and improvements. Indexation only applies to costs from 1 July 2027, but the cost base still matters for the gain before then.
  • Save the value of each asset at 30 June 2027: a holding statement for shares, and for property a valuation or at least the comparable sales from around then.
  • Don't sell early just to keep the discount. Growth to 30 June 2027 keeps it anyway, whenever you sell.
