The 2026-27 tax cut and $1,000 standard deduction: what they're worth

18 November 2026

Two changes from 1 July 2026 lower the tax on a wage. The rate on income between $18,201 and $45,000 dropped from 16% to 15%. And employees get a $1,000 standard deduction for work-related expenses without keeping receipts. A third change is legislated for 1 July 2027, when the 15% rate drops to 14% (ATO tax rates).

The rate cut: $268 a year, at most

One percentage point on the $26,800 of income between $18,200 and $45,000 is $268. Anyone earning $45,000 or more gets exactly that, whatever their salary. That's about $5.15 a week, and your employer's withholding has included it since your first pay in July.

Income tax plus the 2% Medicare levy, after the low income tax offset, before the standard deduction. The 2026-27 and 2027-28 Medicare levy thresholds for low incomes haven't been published yet. That only matters below about $35,000, so it doesn't change any row here.

The $1,000 standard deduction

From 2026-27 the ATO takes up to $1,000 off the taxable income of resident employees automatically when you lodge (ATO). It isn't in your pay. It comes back as part of your refund.

What it's worth depends on your tax rate:

  • $220 at $45,000
  • $335 at $60,000 (a little more, because a lower taxable income also gets back some of the low income tax offset)
  • $320 from about $67,000 to $135,000 (30% plus the 2% Medicare levy)
  • $470 at $200,000 (45% plus 2%)

It replaces claiming work-related expenses, it doesn't add to them. If your working from home, car, tools and other work costs come to more than $1,000, claim them instead and keep the records. The work deductions calculator shows which way is better for you. Union and professional association fees can be claimed on top.

Together

For someone on $100,000 the two changes are worth $588 for 2026-27: $268 through the year in their pay and $320 at tax time. Take-home pay after tax and Medicare, with the deduction, is $77,800, or about $1,496 a week. From July 2027 the rate cut doubles to $536 a year compared with 2025-26.
